Security · be honest about limitations

This login is a basic privacy layer, not real security. It exists to keep casual viewers away from JLZ data — not to stop a determined attacker.

What it does:

  • Prevents someone glancing at your laptop from seeing data immediately
  • Locks the screen after 30 minutes of inactivity
  • Stores passwords as SHA-256 hashes (salted)
  • Cooldown after 5 failed attempts

What it does NOT do:

  • The HTML source contains the password hashes — someone with technical knowledge can extract them
  • Local data (localStorage) is NOT encrypted — anyone with file system access can read it
  • No server-side validation, no rate limiting that survives refresh

For real security (multi-user roles, audit logs, encrypted storage), the tool would need a proper backend (Cloudflare Workers, Vercel + Auth, etc.).

How this works definitions · methodology
Inventory snapshot
The starting point. How many lbs you had on a specific date. Everything in the projection is calculated from this number forward.
Demand baseline
The weekly average of cases sold over the last N weeks. We use this to forecast how much you'll sell going forward. 8 weeks is the default — long enough to smooth noise, short enough to reflect recent trends.
Expected shrink
Roughly 14% of every container is lost between arrival and packing (defects, mold, processing). When we recommend "order X cases", we're already accounting for this — so X is the gross amount you need to buy, not the net amount you'll sell.
Safety stock
How many weeks of demand you want to keep as buffer. Default 2.5 weeks reflects the actual demand variability in JLZ's last 26 weeks (CV ≈ 55%, weekly demand ranging from 506 to 3,325 cases). Going below safety is a risk, not panic — it means a single late container could leave you short. Going below zero is the actual problem.
Order cadence
How often to place a new order. Each container holds enough net product (after shrink) to cover roughly one week of demand, so a 1-week cadence keeps warehouse time minimal. Faster cadence → less shrink, more shipping cost; slower cadence → fewer trips but more time sitting (more shrink). The suggested value is computed from your demand baseline.
Lead time stages
Sea (Hueneme) total ≈ 37 days, broken into: Harvest+processing 12d (vendor cosechas against the PO — varies if they already have product on hand), Inland to port 4d (truck from Satipo/Pichanaqui to Callao + carrier cut-off window for FCL), Ocean transit 18d (Callao → Hueneme), Port→WH 3d. Air (LAX) total ≈ 13d: Harvest 10d + Inland to airport 3d (truck to Lima + airline cut-off). Flight is same-day so it's not a separate stage. All stage values editable in Settings.
Sea vs Air
Sea is roughly 3× cheaper than Air. We always default to Sea. Air is only recommended when the timing of a stockout doesn't allow Sea to arrive in time. A common play is 1 air now + sea for the rest.
Pipeline
Orders that have been placed but haven't arrived yet (status: Contracted or In Transit, from the Orders tab). Their ETAs are factored into the projection.
Stockout vs Low
Stockout means ending stock that week is at or below zero. Low means below the safety threshold. Both trigger action.
